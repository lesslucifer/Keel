The problem

When you sign a waived review, your signature covers only its reason. Its counts, open findings and seam waivers can change after you sign and the gate does not notice. The waiver and your sign-off are stored one after the other, so a failure between them leaves a waiver nobody signed. The gate prints recorded text as is, so a crafted reason can redraw your terminal. A session can still write a waiver with any reason. And some tests cannot fail on the bug they guard.

Terms

WaiverA review recorded as skipped or stopped, with a reason. Yours, typed at sign-off, or the cap's, written by the tool after the last round.
Signed lineWhat your sign-off binds about the waiver. The gate rebuilds it from the review record and compares.

The work

B17 · The waiver you sign

In short: the sign-off binds the whole waiver, stores it with the waiver in one step, and the gate prints recorded text inert.

  • CrNote::waiver_hash hashes a canonical text: a version line, the reason, the blockers and importants counts, unreviewed, each open finding and each walk waiver (compact JSON, note order). The review: WAIVED sha256:… line in the payload keeps its shape.
  • Check 8 names the cause when the signed hash is the old reason-only hash: signed by an older keel, re-sign.
  • keel mr confirm stores the cr and mr notes in one ref transaction: each note goes first to a pending ref under refs/notes/keel-pending-… (git refuses notes outside refs/notes/), then one git update-ref --stdin moves both real refs, checked against their old values, and deletes the pending refs. A failure deletes the pending refs and leaves both real refs as they were.
  • gate.rs print(): every failure text and fix, and every recorded reason in a WAIVED line, goes through the sign-off listing's printable() (a fix keeps its own line breaks; each line is escaped).

B18 · No reasoned waiver from a session

In short: the writer takes no --reason. The cap's waiver, whose reason the tool writes, and your waiver at sign-off remain.

  • keel notes write cr refuses --reason with every verdict; the refusal names the cap's form and yours. CrWrite::Waiver and the waiver's typed counts go.
  • Tests that wrote a waiver through the writer move to the sign-off (--waive-review), or plant a cap-shaped note by hand in their scratch repo where they test accepting a tool's waiver.

B16 · The six round-3 nits

In short: five small fixes and one kept as is.

  1. The --target no-such-ref test asserts the error names the target. That CLI path fails at the merge-base before the cap is read, so the "names no commit" rule gets a unit test of its own (the resolution split out of max_rounds).
  2. The history tag of a fix-PASS says when its note records another round than the line gives it, as a reviewed round's tag already does.
  3. A fix-PASS over a tip whose note is a waiver with no review under it (yours) says so and names the next step, not "the step the writer printed".
  4. The fix-PASS writer reads the tip's cr note once: the schedule keeps the tip's own entry instead of dropping it. The cap's waiver uses the same entry.
  5. keel mr confirm reads the tip's cr note once: the gate's read when the review is judged, the history's read when you waive it; the history takes the gate's note instead of reading it again.
  6. The one diff-tree per commit stays (open point 2).

B22 · Directive tests that can fail

In short: each directive test adds an unquoted label after the directive and asserts the check names it.

  • tests/verify_doc.rs a_quoted_diagram_with_a_directive_is_green and src/verify_doc.rs a_directive_is_skipped_whole_multi_line_or_inline.
