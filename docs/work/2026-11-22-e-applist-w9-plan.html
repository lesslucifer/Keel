The problem

Each part of the app is tested alone. Nothing yet shows that a router line reaches the screen and an alert. The real Mac alert, its click and the Telegram link have never been seen on the installed app. And what the epic built is written only in working notes that leave at the close.

What you get

  • The epic test: one command runs the real app window on a throwaway project. It plays the router and checks each step on screen.
  • One check of the installed app, by you, about twenty minutes.
  • The lasting docs: the app page, the install page and the map say what was built. Two new use cases, one decision record, the backlog row closed.

How the test runs

  1. It builds the window's code in seconds. Nothing is packaged or signed.
  2. It opens the app on a temp project and a temp app folder. Your own Keel is untouched.
  3. The real router scripts write a plan to read and an alert. The plan appears; one alert is recorded.
  4. Open hands over the committed page. Mark sent survives a restart, and no alert repeats.
  5. A review note, a sign-off and a merge make a result appear and leave.
  6. A router restart, then silence: the router's items go, then "router not heard".

Your other test commands stay as fast as today.

What it makes

  • The test, its throwaway world, and a quick check of that world that runs on every push.
  • Doc updates. You commit the three app pages yourself, as rule files.

Left to the epic's close: moving the working notes out of git, the epic report, the review, your sign-off and the merge.
