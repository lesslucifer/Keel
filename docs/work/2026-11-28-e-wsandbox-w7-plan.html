The problem

The router and the watchdog run with your rights, outside any sandbox. One guard script limits them. Dead workers and finished worktrees pile up until you close them by hand.

C5, by example

At every turn end a hook checks all docs and refuses while one is red. A worker fixes the doc. The router cannot, so it retries. On 10 November a router was refused 332 times in 21 minutes.

C6, by example

The watchdog's schedule ends 7 days after it starts. Started Monday, it goes silent next Monday. Silence also means "no workers", so a stuck worker goes unreported. From day 6 it tells the router to restart it.

What W7 builds

Your picks, each behind a test that fails first, staged for your one commit. W5 changes how a worker stops, so W5 merges first and W7 builds on it. Your W7 commit waits for W5.

How you'll know it worked

  • The router closes a dead worker and cleans up with one command.
  • The watchdog names each finished worktree and the step that closes it.
  • Nothing you did not pick changed.
