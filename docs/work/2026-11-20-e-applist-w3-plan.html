E-APPLIST.W3 · The alert and stage lines, and the app's link

The router's side of your desk alert: what it writes to its update log, and when.

T3 · WU plan · 2026-11-21 · Keel · its data file, 2026-11-20-e-applist-w3-plan.yaml, sits beside this page · the epic: E-APPLIST plan

The problem

The update log exists, but nothing writes alerts to it yet. The router's Telegram message names no item the app could open. The router has no rule for when to say a work unit moved on, or when a question is answered.

What you get

  • Every Telegram message from the router also leaves one alert line, written first, so a Telegram failure never loses the desk alert.
  • A message about a work unit ends with a link that opens it in the app.
  • A question from the router is listed until you answer it.
  • The router writes each work unit's stage at a fixed moment: plan in work, plan to read, plan approved, building, in review.

How it works

  1. The router writes its message with two optional first lines: the work unit, and a question's short name.
  2. The sender drops those lines, writes the question line, then the alert line: the headline, one or two lines, the work unit and its link.
  3. Then it sends to Telegram, the link as the last line, such as keel://wu/E-APPLIST.W3.
  4. If the log cannot be written, the message still goes, and the sender says why.
  5. A mistyped first line stops everything: nothing written, nothing sent.

What needs you

  1. Three typed rules commits: the sender, the router skill (both copies), the project's router notes. A fresh reviewer re-reads each before you sign.
  2. Later, at W6's close: one Telegram message, to see whether the link opens the app.

What it makes

  • The sender's new step, with its tests.
  • The router skill's moments for each stage, the log's start, and deleting a stage or a question.
  • Tests that keep the two skill copies equal and the router notes' rule-file list complete.
